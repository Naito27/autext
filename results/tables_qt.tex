\caption{The reduced scheduling problem and the two solvers on it. Each row is
one pair of local codes $(C_c,C'_c)$ of generator weight $w$; $|E|$ and $\Delta$
are the edge count and maximum degree of $T^{(\rm base)}_{\rm QT}$. A class is
one inequivalent column permutation of the index-1 matrices $H_1,H'_1$, and the
three outcome columns partition the total: the greedy reaches $\Delta$; the
greedy fails but CP-SAT reaches $\Delta$; or CP-SAT proves $\Delta$ infeasible.
The last pair of columns is the amortized cost $t_1{+}t_2$ of the left--right
circuit of Ref.~[24] on the same base graph, given as its range over the classes
and the number of classes on which it reaches $\Delta$. It never does so once
$w\geq9$. \emph{Caveat:} a class here is a distinct \emph{code}, which is
Ref.~[36]'s dedup and is the right one for $(n,k,d)$; the base graph however
depends on the \emph{matrices}, and permutations giving the same code can give
inequivalent graphs. For $[8,4,4]\times[2,1,2]$ the $8!$ column permutations
give 1680 distinct matrix pairs falling into 7 relabelling-invariant types, of
which these 30 classes touch 2, so the sweep is a sample of the orientation
space rather than an exhaustive enumeration of it. A sample of 120 graphs
outside the 30 reached $\Delta$ in every case (116 by the greedy, 4 by
CP-SAT), but their $t_{\rm LR}$ reaches 11, outside the range quoted here.}
\label{tab:qt-base}
